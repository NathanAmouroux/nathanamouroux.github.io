Nathan Amouroux

Cosmologist, Ph.D. in subatomic and astroparticle physics

I work to make large sky surveys reliable enough for cosmology. At the Vera C. Rubin Observatory I characterized the telescope’s photometric response with a Collimated Beam Projector, and for LSST DESC I built the tools that mask bright stars and test how well galaxy clusters are detected.

Research

Photometric calibration

I developed the analysis chain for the Collimated Beam Projector, an in-dome light source that sends point sources of known wavelength and flux into the telescope. It gave the first in-situ measurements of five LSST filters and of detector quantum efficiency.

Read more

Bright-star masking

Bright foreground stars bias galaxy photometry and create false detections. I wrote the software that builds masks around them; it is now used in LSST DESC, including on Rubin Data Preview 1.

Read more

Galaxy clusters

Clusters only constrain cosmology if their catalogs are well understood. I measured the completeness and purity of the AMICO cluster finder on simulated LSST data, as a function of redshift and halo mass.

L’Odyssée Cosmique de Pénélope

Comic book, in preparation with CNRS Éditions

A comic book about the work of PhD students, built around six theses in cosmology and astroparticle physics from LAPP and LAPTh. I have led the project since 2024.

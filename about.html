About

I am a cosmologist working at the interface of astronomical instrumentation, data processing and cosmological analysis. I did my Ph.D. at the Laboratoire d’Annecy de Physique des Particules (LAPP), within the Vera C. Rubin Observatory calibration effort and the LSST Dark Energy Science Collaboration (DESC), supervised by Thibault Guillemin.

My thesis had three complementary threads in the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST): characterizing the telescope’s photometric response with a Collimated Beam Projector, limiting the impact of bright objects on galaxy samples, and validating galaxy cluster detection. They share one question: how do we turn ever larger astronomical datasets into precise and reliable measurements of the Universe?

I studied physics at Université Grenoble Alpes (a Magistère in fundamental physics, along with a master’s in subatomic physics and cosmology), after two years of preparatory classes in Montpellier. My internships went from crowd dynamics studies of micro-swimming algae at the Laboratoire Interdisciplinaire de Physique, Grenoble, to gravitational lensing by galaxy clusters at the Laboratoire de Physique Subatomique et de Cosmologie, Grenoble, to cluster detection for Rubin at LAPP.

I am a member of LSST DESC and LSST France (since 2023) and of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory commissioning and calibration team (since 2024).

Beyond the lab

I run, ride a road bike, swim and hike. I play video games, enjoy mechanics and DIY projects, and I am a beginner in amateur astronomy. I also like explaining research to the public: see outreach, including the comic book I co-authored.
